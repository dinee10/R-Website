Literature survey

Plant identification: Leafsnap and other image-based systems show how computer vision can identify plant species [11], [12]. A Sri Lankan mobile tool also combined herb identification with learning content [13].

Herbal knowledge: An ontology was developed to represent Sri Lankan medicinal-plant information [8]. A Sinhala virtual assistant made this knowledge more conversational [9], while research on hallucinations highlights the need to check generated answers [10].

Quality assessment: EfficientNetV2 provides a foundation for image classification [14]. Grad-CAM helps explain image predictions by highlighting influential image regions [15].

Cultivation recommendation: Research on Sri Lankan medicinal materials identifies sourcing and quality challenges [2]. The group thesis highlights the gap in recommendations combining soil, weather, land and budget for small-scale medicinal-plant farmers.

Research gap

Existing tools often address identification, herbal knowledge, plant quality or cultivation separately. Few connect these functions with bilingual explanations and safety checks for Sri Lankan medicinal plants.

Research problem

How can an integrated AI system improve medicinal-plant identification, reliable knowledge access, quality assessment and cultivation decisions in Sri Lanka?

Objectives

Develop and evaluate a mobile system that identifies medicinal plants, provides grounded Sinhala and English knowledge, explains leaf quality and recommends suitable plants to grow.

Research components

01 · IT22168986

Plant Identification

EfficientNetV2 classifiers and YOLO11 detect and identify plants from leaf, seed and flower images.

02 · IT22640598

Herbal Knowledge

A bilingual RAG assistant retrieves curated plant information and provides answers with precautions.

03 · IT22132796

Cultivation Recommendation

A machine-learning recommendation module considers soil, land size, budget and local weather.

04 · IT22112132

Quality Assessment

An EfficientNetV2-B0 cascade checks species, condition and maturity, with Grad-CAM explanations.

Methodology

The team followed literature review, data collection, preprocessing, model development, component integration and evaluation, using a shared Flutter application and Flask API.

Technologies

Flutter, Python and Flask support the application. The research uses EfficientNetV2, YOLO11, Grad-CAM, ChromaDB, OpenAI text-embedding-3-large and GPT-4o mini, alongside machine-learning models for cultivation.

Reported findings

Results summarized from the Group Final Thesis Draft.

01 · Plant Identification

93.33% leaf validation

14/15 leaf, 59/59 seed or spice, and 32/35 flower images were correctly classified in recorded validation. Small splits and duplicate images limit generalization.

02 · Herbal Knowledge

11/12 correct retrievals

Correct plant knowledge was retrieved for 5/6 English and 6/6 Sinhala questions. All eight audience-interaction tests passed.

03 · Cultivation Recommendation

Prototype tested

Recommendations were tested with soil, land, budget and weather inputs. The cultivation dataset remains too small for a dependable general accuracy claim.

04 · Quality Assessment

89.27% condition accuracy

Held-out testing reported 96.66% species, 89.27% condition and 91.74% maturity accuracy, with Grad-CAM visual explanations.

Results are component-level and do not establish clinical use or field accuracy for every setting.
